Digital Drug Safety CardMFDS R&D · 2022-2024

Three years spent turning the paper drug safety card into a QR digital card and getting drug allergy information to travel between hospitals in a standard form

Introduction video 2 min · narrated, with subtitles

A patient who has had a drug allergy or a severe adverse drug reaction meets the same risk again at every new hospital. This project standardized the issuing criteria and the data elements of the drug safety card patients carry, and turned it into a digital drug safety card whose QR code opens the full detail. At the same time we built a standard form for exchanging adverse reaction information between hospitals, plus the sharing platform myadr.care, and ran it in 9 university hospitals. The work ran over three annual phases from February 2022 to December 2024 with support from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. Chungbuk National University Hospital was the lead institution, with the Seoul National University R&DB Foundation and Kakao Healthcare as partners. The sharing standard set here was adopted as the standard for the Ministry of Health and Welfare drug allergy information sharing program.

Problem
The old drug safety card has to be on the patient at all times, and one card can hardly convey which drugs to avoid. Card forms and issuing criteria differed from hospital to hospital, so few institutions actually used a card issued somewhere else, and issuing itself happened mostly at general hospitals that host a Regional Pharmacovigilance Center. A National Health Insurance Service analysis estimated 430,000 patients with adverse drug reactions in 2014 alone, and KRW 535.2 billion in socioeconomic loss.

What we built

Digital Drug Safety Card

We reviewed the existing paper and plastic drug safety cards and the European Drug Allergy Passport, then designed a new form. A QR code sits on the front, and the card shows the culprit drug and the symptoms along with the contraindicated, caution and safe-to-take drugs, like a traffic light. Opening the QR code brings up the full detail, down to causality and severity.

Standard drug safety card form

We settled on two forms: a general one carrying every data element of the Korea Institute of Drug Safety and Risk Management (KIDS) drug safety card, and a dedicated one for contrast media, where allergic reactions are common. Depending on what suits the hospital, a site can choose a form that prints the reconciliation information on the card itself, or a form that carries only the adverse event and sends the rest to the QR code.

Standard form for drug allergy information sharing

We defined the data elements needed to move adverse reaction information between hospitals and mapped them to HL7 FHIR AllergyIntolerance. On top of the onset date, culprit drug, symptoms, severity and causality of the adverse event, we added reconciliation elements as extensions: contraindicated, caution and safe-to-take drug groups. Adverse event names follow MedDRA and severity follows CTCAE.

My ADR platform (myadr.care)

A web platform that carries the standard form as it is. It handles patient registration, culprit drug lookup, and the entry and review of adverse reaction information, and works with KD codes, ATC codes and SNOMED CT codes side by side. A template feature prefills the data elements each institution uses, and from a saved record you can print a PDF card, issue a QR card or send a KakaoTalk alert on the spot. We ran the test server and the service server separately and published the user manual on GitBook.

Patient mobile app and KakaoTalk alerts

Working with Kakao Healthcare, we built an app on the MyData model, where the patient holds and reads their own adverse reaction record. For patients who find apps hard to use, we added a second route that sends the digital drug safety card image and link by KakaoTalk alert.

Passport on Drug Allergy GPT

A generative language model demo with the standard form and the shared data elements written into its instructions. Upload an allergy information file mapped to FHIR and it answers from that file, showing whether a given drug falls under contraindicated, caution or safe to take by ATC code. It can also translate the card into other languages, Chinese included.

Rollout led by Regional Pharmacovigilance Centers

Using the Chungbuk National University Hospital workflow as the reference process, we widened participation along two tracks: the Regional Pharmacovigilance Centers and the Korean Society of Health-System Pharmacists. Card issuing differs by hospital, so we wrote down and fixed a process for each site, from putting a QR sticker on a physical card to sending the link by KakaoTalk alert alone. We also piloted a link that fills in the sharing form inside the EMR and posts it to the platform over an API.

Adopted as the standard for a Ministry of Health and Welfare program

The adverse drug reaction sharing standard built in this project was adopted as the standard for the Ministry of Health and Welfare pilot program on a drug allergy information sharing system between institutions. We also proposed adding drug allergy to the allergy and intolerance section of the Health Information Highway, and an upgrade to the allergy elements of the Korean Core Data for Interoperability (KR-CDI) held by the Korea Health Information Service.

Timeline

2022.02Project start. We extended the common model for issuing drug safety cards, built in earlier work, to 5 institutions, and designed a new common model with a steering committee and expert interviews.
2022.12Principal Investigator Min-Gyu Kang received a commendation from the Minister of Health and Welfare at the 6th Healthcare Data Innovation Forum.
2023.03We submitted to the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety a proposed amendment to the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act that would give each patient their own adverse reaction information.
2023When security rules blocked building the database inside KIDS, we built our own MVP platform with the same standard elements and database structure and piloted it at Chungbuk National University Hospital. New sites joined, among them Jeonbuk National University Hospital.
2024.03We presented the myadr.care platform and the issuing process at the Regional Pharmacovigilance Center directors' meeting held at Chungnam National University Hospital.
2024We split the test server from the service server and put the service platform into full operation, and piloted the patient mobile app and the EMR API link. Each hospital established its own process for issuing the QR-based digital drug safety card.
2024.10An awareness survey of 1,000 members of the public was published in BMC Public Health, and we presented the digital drug safety card results to the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety and the Ministry of Health and Welfare.
2024.12Project ended. 709 patients had registered on the platform across 9 university hospitals, and 372 more patients registered at 5 hospitals after the project closed (through 2025.4).
2025.02Submitted the final report.
2025.04Held the final review meeting. After the project closed, 372 more patients registered at 5 hospitals.
